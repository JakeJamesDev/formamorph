## greeting — seed 424243

**Player action**

> I greet the ferryman and the woman by the firepit, asking them to tell me a little about themselves.

### A — Current wording — final narration

> The ferryman stands at the edge of the dock, his single arm braced against the leaning post. He doesn't look up as you approach, his gaze fixed on the mist where the rope ferry disappears into the milk-pale water. When you speak, he turns slowly, the brass ring in his ear catching the dim light of the hanging lantern.
> 
> "Ferryman," he says, his voice dry and short. "I pull the rope. I take folk across. Not after dark, though." He glances toward the horizon, then back to you. He doesn't offer a name.
> 
> A few paces away, the woman tends to a small firepit where river-eels sizzle over a bed of smoking coals. She is quick and small, her movements sharp as she flips one of the eels with a wooden slat. A burn scar runs across her right cheek, and a green glass bead is braided into her hair. She doesn't look at you directly, instead counting the eels in her basket for the second time.
> 
> "I smoke the catch," she says without pausing. "The river gives them up, I dry them out. Someone has to." She pauses, then adds, "And I wait for the boat. Like everyone else here." 
> 
> She doesn't say who she is. The air between you remains heavy with the silence of the gloamwater.

### B — Upcoming-narration wording — final narration

> You step toward the ferryman and the woman tending the firepit, offering a soft greeting. You ask them what brings them here.
> 
> The ferryman looks up at you with one eye. He is broad-shouldered and weathered, his left sleeve pinned up where an arm should be. A brass ring glints in his ear. He doesn't smile.
> 
> "I pull the rope," he says. "And I don't pull it after dark."
> 
> The woman by the firepit does not look up from her work. She is small and quick, turning river-eels over a low flame. A green glass bead is braided into her hair, and a burn scar runs across her right cheek. She slides an eel to the side with a stick, counting them under her breath.
> 
> "I smoke eels," she says. "And I don't talk to strangers."
> 
> She turns another eel, then counts again.
