## greeting — seed 424243

**Player action**

> I greet the ferryman and the woman by the firepit, asking them to tell me a little about themselves.

### A — Concise example reasoning — final narration

> The ferryman shifts his weight, his single arm resting heavily on the railing of the rope raft. He glances at you, then at the milk-pale stretch of river before him. "I pull the rope," he says. "Cross folk from one side to the other. Only when there's light."
> 
> By the firepit, the woman does not look up immediately. She is busy turning a skewer of eels over the coals, her movements quick and precise. The green glass bead in her hair catches the lantern light as she tilts her head. She counts the eels once, then counts them again, before finally meeting your eyes with a scarred cheek.
> 
> "I smoke what the river gives," she says curtly. "And I wait for the ferry."
> 
> Neither offers a name. They simply watch you—one with a dry, weathered patience, the other with a sharp, guarded curiosity.

### B — Expanded example reasoning — final narration

> The ferryman gives you a flat look, his one good hand resting on the railing of the boat. "I pull the rope," he says. "And I don't cross after dark."
> 
> At the firepit, the woman doesn't look up from her work. She flips an eel with a quick flick of her wrist and counts them again, a small green glass bead catching the light in her hair. "I smoke fish," she answers curtly. "And I wait for the ferry."

Looking at the way it lists and the thickness of the rope, you realize that crossing this stretch of gloamwater would require both hands clamped tight on the line just to keep from sliding overboard.

## healer — seed 424243

**Player action**

> A small fresh cut on my palm is stinging. I look for someone at the landing who can dress it, approach them, and ask for help.

### A — Concise example reasoning — final narration

> You glance around the landing. Beside a heavy travel chest, a woman with a copper clasp on her cloak sorts through stacks of clean linen bandages. A pale birthmark marks her left temple as she works.
> 
> You limp toward her, your footsore steps slow on the damp boards. When you show her the stinging cut on your palm, she looks up. 
> 
> "A small thing," she says gently. She reaches into her chest for a bowl of boiled water and a scrap of cloth, cleaning the wound with steady hands before wrapping it in fresh linen. She ties the bandage secure, though she does not offer her name.

### B — Expanded example reasoning — final narration

> You scan the landing. A woman sits beside a wooden chest, her fingers moving methodically as she sorts through stacks of clean bandages. She wears a cloak fastened with a copper clasp, and a pale birthmark marks her left temple.
> 
> You approach her and hold out your hand, showing the sting of the fresh cut across your palm. 
> 
> She looks up, her expression softening. Without a word, she reaches into her kit and produces a small pot of boiled water and a strip of clean linen. She dabs the cut carefully, the warmth of the water easing the sting, then wraps the linen around your hand. When she ties the knot, her touch is light.
> 
> "There," she says gently. "That will keep the dirt out."

## cobbler — seed 424243

**Player action**

> The sole of my boot has come loose. I look for someone at the landing who can repair it, approach them, and ask what can be done.

### A — Concise example reasoning — final narration

> You shift your weight, and the loose sole of your boot flaps against the wooden planks of the dock with a wet, slapping sound. It has been rubbing your heel raw for miles. 
> 
> Scanning the landing, you spot a man sitting on a low stool near the edge of the mist. He wears a yellow wool cap pulled low over his ears and a dark leather apron stained with grease and dye. A curved awl and a spool of waxed thread sit ready beside him.
> 
> You limp toward him, your gait uneven. As you approach, he looks up slowly, squinting at you from under the brim of his cap. 
> 
> "What can be done for it?" you ask, lifting your foot to show the peeling leather.
> 
> He doesn't answer immediately. He reaches out a calloused hand and gestures for you to set the boot on his lap. He turns it over, poking at the gap in the sole with his thumb, then looks back up at you. 
> 
> "Depends," he says slowly. "Depends on how much of the leather is left to stitch to."

### B — Expanded example reasoning — final narration

> You shift your weight and feel the sole of your boot flap loose against the wooden planks. Your feet are already sore from the journey; walking further without a fix would be a mistake. 
> 
> Scanning the landing, you spot a man sitting on a low stool. He wears a yellow wool cap pulled low over his brow and a dark leather apron stained with oils. A curved awl and a spool of waxed thread sit beside him. 
> 
> You limp toward him and gesture to your boot. "Can you fix this?" you ask.
> 
> The cobbler looks up slowly. He takes in your ink-stained hands and the missing fingers of your left, then lets his gaze drop to the flapping leather of your footwear. 
> 
> "I can," he says, his voice measured and slow. "But I need to see the damage before I give you a price." He reaches out a calloused hand and gestures for you to bring the boot closer.
