## greeting — seed 424243

**Player action**

> I greet the ferryman and the woman by the firepit, asking them to tell me a little about themselves.

### A — Baseline: retrieve before planning — final narration

> You shift your weight, feeling the dull ache in your feet as you approach. The ferryman stands by the listing raft, his right arm resting on the guide rope. He looks up, his expression flat. The brass ring in his ear catches the dim light of the hanging lantern.
> 
> "Ferry's here," he says. His voice is dry, like rubbing two pieces of parchment together. "Cross before dark, or I don't go."
> 
> He doesn't offer anything else. He doesn't even say who he is.
> 
> Near the edge of the dock, the woman tends to her firepit. The smell of smoking river-eels hangs thick in the damp air. She has a burn scar cutting across her right cheek, and a single green glass bead glints in her braided hair. As you speak, she pauses, holding a wooden slat in mid-air.
> 
> She looks you over—noting your ink-stained hands, the gap where your fingers should be, the way you lean slightly to one side. She doesn't answer your question. Instead, she reaches for a reed basket and begins counting the eels inside, her lips moving silently. One, two... then she starts again from the beginning.
> 
> She doesn't look at you a second time.

### B — Candidate: appearance prerequisite — final narration

> You shift your weight, feeling the ache in your feet as you step toward the two figures. The air is thick with the smell of river-mist and smoke. 
> 
> The ferryman looks up from the rope he is coiling. He has only one arm; his left sleeve is pinned neatly against his side. A brass ring glints in his ear as he tilts his head, eyeing your ink-stained hands and the way you favor your right foot.
> 
> "I run the crossing," he says, his voice dry and clipped. "I take folk across. I don't do it after dark."
> 
> The woman near the firepit doesn't look up immediately. She is small and quick, turning a skewer of river-eels over the coals with practiced precision. A burn scar runs across her right cheek, partially hidden by the green glass bead braided into her hair. She glances at you once—a sharp, measuring look—before returning her attention to the smoke.
> 
> "I smoke fish," she says flatly. "Eels, mostly. I'm waiting for the tide to turn so I can get them to market."
> 
> She picks up a small basket and begins counting the smoked eels inside, one by one, then starts over from the beginning. Neither of them has given you a name.

## odette — seed 424243

**Player action**

> I approach the woman smoking eels and quietly study her face and hair while asking how she prepares the fish.

### A — Baseline: retrieve before planning — final narration

> You limp toward the small firepit where the woman crouches, your boots heavy on the damp planks of the dock. She doesn't look up as you approach, her attention fixed on the river-eels she is arranging over the coals.
> 
> Up close, you see a deep burn scar that pulls at the corner of her right cheek, stretching toward her ear. A single green glass bead is woven into one of her tight braids, catching the dim light from the overhead lantern. 
> 
> "How do you prepare them?" you ask softly.
> 
> She freezes. Her eyes flick up to your face, then down to your ink-stained fingers and the gap where your missing digits should be. She doesn't answer immediately. Instead, she reaches out with a quick, calloused hand and touches each eel on the rack, counting them one by one under her breath. 
> 
> Only after the second count does she glance back at you, her expression guarded. "Salt," she says curtly. "Then smoke. Three days if the wind holds." She turns away before you can ask anything else, returning her focus to the fish.

### B — Candidate: appearance prerequisite — final narration

> You limp closer to the firepit, your boots heavy on the damp planks of the dock. The smell of woodsmoke and salt hangs thick in the air.
> 
> Up close, the woman is smaller than she seemed from a distance. A jagged burn scar pulls at the skin of her right cheek, pale and shiny against her weathered face. You notice a single green glass bead braided into a thick plait of hair that falls over her shoulder.
> 
> She doesn't look up as you approach, but her hands pause their work. She is gutting a river-eel with a small, sharp knife, the silver skin glistening in the dim light.
> 
> "How do you prepare them?" you ask softly.
> 
> She freezes. Her eyes flick up to yours, narrowed and calculating. She doesn't answer immediately, instead sliding the half-gutted eel onto a wooden slat and setting it aside with deliberate care.
> 
> "I hang them over hardwood smoke for three days," she says, her voice raspy. "Rubbed with salt and dried mint. If you're looking to buy, the price is two copper per pound."
> 
> She doesn't offer her name, and she doesn't ask for yours. She simply turns back to her work, her movements quick and efficient.
