The rain had not stopped since dawn, and the ferry landing smelled of tar and wet rope. Mira Calloway stood under the toll house eaves with her collar turned up, watching the river climb the pilings one board at a time. Beside her, the old ferryman counted coins into a tin box and pretended not to listen.

"You said the courier would be here by noon," she said.

"I said the courier would be here," Tobias answered, without looking up. "I never promised the river would agree."

A bell rang twice from the far bank. Through the gray curtain of rain, a flat-bottomed boat pushed off from the opposite pier, its lantern swinging. Mira stepped to the edge of the planks. The courier was a narrow young woman in an oilskin coat, and she held a leather satchel against her chest as though it were a sleeping child.

When the boat bumped against the landing, the courier did not wait for the rope. She jumped the gap, slipped on the wet boards, and caught herself on Mira's arm.

"Calloway?" the young woman asked, breathless. "They told me you would have the second key."

Mira nodded and drew a brass key from her coat. Tobias finally closed his tin box. He looked at the satchel, then at the two women, and his weathered face went very still.

"Whatever is in that bag," he said quietly, "it is not going across my river again."

The courier tightened her grip. "Then we had better open it here."
